Learning by making:
my first 3D printer.

27.10.2026 · Milan Jankovic

The best way to understand a new tool is to make something with it before you know exactly what you’re doing.

Getting my first 3D printer felt a little like bringing a tiny workshop home. There was a machine to assemble, software to learn, and a surprising number of ways for a print to go slightly wrong.

Start with something small

My first prints were deliberately simple. Each one taught me something: how the first layer behaves, how small changes in temperature affect the result, and why patience is part of the process.

That is what I like about making things physically. The feedback is immediate and honest. A model either fits, moves, or it doesn’t. You adjust, try again, and slowly build a better intuition.

Still learning

I’m keeping this as an ongoing note rather than a definitive guide. There are still settings to understand, objects to design, and plenty of failed prints to learn from.
